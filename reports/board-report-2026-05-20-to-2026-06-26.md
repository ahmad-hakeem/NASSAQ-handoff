# NASSAQ Platform — Executive Progress Report
### Reporting Period: 20 May 2026 – 26 June 2026
### Prepared for: Board of Directors

---

## 1. Executive Summary

During the period from **20 May to 26 June 2026**, the NASSAQ platform underwent an
intensive phase of stabilization, security hardening, and feature completion. The work
was not cosmetic and was not idle: the engineering record shows **more than 600 individual
changes and 29 production releases** shipped to live users across the period.

The bulk of the effort was driven by **three categories of real, platform-impacting problems**:

1. **Security and data-isolation defects** — several issues allowed, or risked allowing,
   one school's data to be visible to another, or allowed lower-privileged accounts to act
   beyond their authority. In a multi-tenant system serving many schools and holding
   sensitive student and family information, these are the most serious class of problem we
   can face, and they were treated accordingly.

2. **Data-correctness defects** — student grades, attendance percentages, class counts, and
   teacher-to-school links were in some cases showing or storing incorrect values. These
   directly affect the trust teachers, principals, and parents place in the numbers the
   platform reports.

3. **Workflow-blocking defects** — certain core daily actions (ending a lesson, recording
   live-session scores, changing a parent password, viewing class and student lists,
   publishing a timetable) were failing for some users. When a workflow is blocked, the
   affected user cannot do their job that day, so these were prioritized for immediate fixes.

**Why the work took significant time.** The majority of the issues fixed were *not* simple
visual mistakes. They were caused by **data-flow mismatches across multiple modules** — for
example, a score recorded in a live session not arriving correctly in the follow-up report,
or an attendance figure calculated from the wrong underlying data source. Diagnosing this
class of problem requires tracing data end-to-end across the database, the backend, and the
frontend, and confirming that each fix does not break a neighboring workflow. Because the
platform is already in production with real schools and real data, every change had to be
made under a strict rule: **production data must never be lost, and existing working flows
must not regress.** This discipline is slower than rebuilding from scratch, but it is the
correct and responsible approach for a live system.

As of 26 June, the platform is **live and serving users**, with the large majority of
discovered issues fully resolved. A small number of items remain in progress or under
verification; these are listed transparently in Section 5.

## 3. High-Impact Problems (Most Serious / Actively Disrupting Use)

These problems were either disrupting live use or carried serious data-protection risk, and
they consumed a disproportionate share of investigation time.

### 3.1 Cross-Tenant Data Isolation (Critical)
**Problem.** In a system serving many schools from one platform, the single most important
guarantee is that **School A can never see School B's data**. We discovered several paths
where this guarantee was weak: the platform-admin "preview as a school" feature and parts of
the notification/communication center could surface data belonging to a different school, and
a request header used for previewing could be abused to widen access.

**Root cause.** Tenant (school) boundaries were not being re-enforced on every read path;
some legacy endpoints trusted that a user was "inside a school" without re-confirming *which*
school for that specific record.

**Fix.** Tenant scoping was enforced consistently across the affected endpoints, the
preview/impersonation mechanism was tightened, notification totals were corrected to be
school-scoped, and **automated regression tests were added** so the leak cannot silently
return.

**Impact.** This is a privacy and trust issue. Left unaddressed, it could have exposed one
school's student and family information to another. Resolving it protected the platform's
core data-protection promise.

### 3.2 Core Data Endpoints Failing (Critical)
**Problem.** For a period, the **class list and student list stopped loading** because the
live database was missing a column that the application code expected.

**Root cause.** A code change introducing "soft delete" (reversible deletion) shipped ahead
of the matching database migration, so the database and the code were briefly out of step.

**Fix.** The missing migration was applied, and a permanent **safeguard was added that blocks
any future deployment where the code expects columns the database does not have.** This turns
a class of silent production failure into a caught-before-release error.

**Impact.** While active, this blocked some of the most-used screens in the product.

### 3.3 End-of-Lesson Workflow and Live-Session Scoring (Critical)
**Problem.** Two of the most-used teacher workflows were failing. **Ending a lesson** could
crash, and the **parent lesson report** ("the teacher ended the class") failed to send for
independent teachers. Separately, **live-session scores** (participation, behavior, skills)
were in some cases arriving in the follow-up report as **all zeros**, and the "undo last
action" button could erase **every** student's points instead of just the last action.

**Root cause.** The scoring pipeline had grown multiple overlapping paths, and the data
recorded during a live session was not flowing reliably into the report and grade ledger.

**Fix.** The scoring pipeline was **unified into a single, backend-owned source of truth**,
the end-of-lesson and parent-report flows were repaired and de-duplicated, and an extensive
set of **automated tests** was added to lock the behavior in place.

**Impact.** These workflows are the heart of the teacher's daily routine. Failures here
directly block teaching staff and corrupt the academic record parents rely on.

### 3.4 Role-Switching, Impersonation, and Account-Takeover Hardening (High)
**Problem.** Administrators can switch roles and preview schools; this is powerful and must
be tightly controlled. We found the controls were inconsistent — some paths lacked
multi-factor verification, audit logging, or proper session revocation — and we found ways
that mutable profile fields could be misused to hijack a teacher identity.

**Fix.** A dedicated security audit was conducted; multi-factor verification was required on
sensitive paths, audit logging was strengthened, stale sessions are now revoked correctly,
and identity fields can no longer be abused for takeover. Known third-party library
vulnerabilities were also patched.

**Impact.** These changes protect against privilege escalation — the scenario where a lower-
privileged or malicious actor gains powers they should not have.

### 3.5 Teacher–School Linkage and Data-Count Integrity (High)
**Problem.** Teachers sometimes disappeared from a principal's teacher list, transfers
between schools got "stuck," duplicate teacher records appeared, and school dashboards showed
**incorrect student/teacher counts**.

**Root cause.** A teacher exists in two related places in the data model, and the two could
drift apart; counts were being "nudged" rather than recalculated from the source of truth.

**Fix.** A single authoritative source for teacher assignments was established, a one-click
resolution was added for stuck transfers, and counts are now **reconciled from real data**
rather than incrementally adjusted, with a routine check that flags any future drift.

**Impact.** Principals and platform admins make decisions based on these numbers and lists;
incorrect data undermines confidence in the whole product.

## 4. Timeline of Work (Chronological)

### 20–26 May — Security audit wave and deletion/restore safety
- Conducted a focused **security audit of role-switching, guardian links, teacher credential
  management, and session lifecycle**; hardened privileged paths and added multi-factor gates.
- Closed **cross-tenant preview leaks** on admin list endpoints and added regression tests.
- Patched **dependency vulnerabilities (CVEs)**.
- Restricted teacher access to **only their assigned classes/students**.
- Introduced **safe, reversible deletion (soft delete)** for classes, students, and subjects,
  with restore flows — and fixed the resulting database-migration gap that briefly broke the
  class and student lists.
- Repaired the **parent password-change crash** and parent-messaging recipient lookup.
- Cleared a wave of **403 "access denied" errors** that were wrongly blocking teachers from
  legitimate pages (portfolio, class metrics, attendance summaries, day status).

### 30 May – 7 June — Frontend toolchain, schema safeguards, and platform-admin fixes
- Stabilized the **frontend build toolchain** and fixed blank-page and modal-layering issues.
- Added a permanent **schema-drift guard** that blocks deploys missing required database columns.
- Fixed the **platform deputy-manager login** (silent failure) and locked down which admin
  pages each platform role may open.
- Began **canonical grade enforcement** across student and class creation to prevent invalid
  grade data.
- Hardened **teacher–school linkage** and shipped one-click resolution for stuck transfers.
- Tightened **notification tenant-scoping** so a newly previewed school no longer shows
  another school's notifications.

### 9–18 June — Live-session scoring, attendance accuracy, and the Independent-Teacher module
- **Unified the live-session scoring pipeline**; fixed all-zero scores in reports and the
  destructive "undo" behavior.
- Corrected **attendance accuracy** across parent and student views (including the parent
  portal showing 100% instead of the true rate) and fixed the underlying data source.
- Delivered Independent-Teacher features: **timetable display, lesson access controls, a
  parents tab, student-report exports (PDF/Excel), and the end-of-lesson parent report.**
- Upgraded the **AI assistant model** and added abuse-rate limiting to public chat.
- Fixed **homework-to-grade syncing** and **per-skill scoring values**.
- Added **manual timetable creation, unpublish, and version history.**

### 21–26 June — Scheduling, attendance unification, notifications, and final hardening
- Made **participation and follow-up-sheet edits persist** reliably across close/reopen.
- **Unified teacher attendance** so live-session attendance writes through to the canonical
  record and agrees with the session summary and report.
- Corrected **AI-Insights alert routing** so each role lands on the right page, with tests.
- Enriched the **standby/coverage workflow**: removed phantom periods, added leadership-
  assigned coverage with teacher notifications, and ensured assignments are saved.
- Fixed **manual-timetable publishing** and isolated lesson plans per teacher and subject.
- Restricted internal identifiers (UUIDs) to platform admins only.
- Diagnosed the **26 June publish failure** as a transient deployment health-check timing
  issue (not a code defect); the application was verified healthy and successfully
  **re-published and is live**.

Throughout the period, **automated tests and regression coverage were added continuously**
alongside fixes, and roughly **29 production releases** were shipped.

  (more required periods than the timetable can physically hold), the generator correctly

  treated as ongoing maintenance, not a one-time task.

  transient health-check timing issue at go-live (a known characteristic of auto-scaling
  cloud deployments). The application boots correctly and is live; we continue to monitor
  release health.

## 6. Management Explanation (for Board Discussion)

**Why the work was extensive.** The platform is a multi-tenant system that holds sensitive
student and family data for multiple schools, with five distinct user types (platform admins,
principals/school admins, teachers, students, and parents) whose permissions and views must
all stay correctly separated. A large share of the issues handled in this period were
**structural data-flow problems** — information being calculated from the wrong source, or
not flowing correctly from one module to another — rather than surface-level visual bugs.
Problems of this kind cannot be fixed by a quick edit; they require tracing data across the
database, the server, and the user interface, and then proving the fix with tests.

**Why the fixes were necessary.** The issues addressed were, in many cases, **blocking real
school operations** — teachers unable to end a lesson or trust their scores, parents seeing
incorrect attendance, principals seeing incomplete teacher lists — or they carried **serious
data-protection risk**, such as one school potentially seeing another's data. These are not
deferrable. For a product whose value rests on accuracy and trust, allowing them to persist
would damage adoption and credibility far more than the cost of fixing them properly.

**Why continuing work was reasonable and expected.** Every change was made under a deliberate
constraint: **do no harm to live data and do not break existing working flows.** This is the
responsible standard for a system already in production. It means each fix is accompanied by
verification and regression tests, and changes to stable logic are made only when strictly
necessary. That discipline is the reason the platform shipped **roughly 29 releases** in five
weeks without data loss, while steadily closing critical and high-severity issues. The small
number of remaining items are documented openly above; none are silent, and none currently
block the platform from operating.

**Bottom line.** The time spent in this period reflects substantive engineering on real,
high-severity platform problems — security, data correctness, and core daily workflows — not
delay without progress. The platform is in materially better shape at the end of the period
than at the start: more secure, more accurate, and more stable, with safeguards now in place
to prevent several whole classes of problem from recurring.
